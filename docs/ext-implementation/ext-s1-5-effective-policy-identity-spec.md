### 3.1 Semantics that must not be lost

- `effective_policy_digest` is **caller-dependent evidence** (contract 4 and 7.4 line 3): it may legitimately differ per caller policy under one unchanged selection epoch; it is bound by the claim commitment and checked only for self-consistency with the claim; it is **never compared to durable state** and never authority.
- `activation_ref` (ADR D-14, contract 4.2) is an immutable reference to the specific consumer-owned activation transition governing the effect: the entry hash of the enforcing component's `activate` log entry, whose hashed content includes the scope key, the selection epoch, the selection digest and the event type. It identifies scope, selection epoch and activation event. It is **not a Registry-issued authorization token**: the registry never produces it and never treats it as authority. There is one canonical representation across Registry, Runtime, Control Plane and evidence: `sha256:` plus 64 lowercase hexadecimal characters, never re-encoded, abbreviated or wrapped, and no component keeps a second form. It is stable across `sync` entries and changes only with a new activation.
- **`BV-001` carries an opaque `effective_policy_digest`.** The contract's worked example prints the earlier payload-only digest, which no effective payload produces under the ruled preimage (section 6). That member is format-checked and never recomputed or checked for self-consistency in `BV-001`; an implementation that does check self-consistency (above) cannot validate `BV-001`, and must treat the vector as an opaque-member control.
- The binding is evidence of what was selected and activated. It authorizes nothing. `operationally_authorized` semantics, grants, approvals, claims and the commit-time check are unchanged.

### 3.2 Fields exempt from the identifier restriction and kept out of the digest

The ASCII-only restriction (section 5.1) applies **only** to the eight identifier members of the binding. It does not apply to, and the binding digest never covers:

- human-readable policy metadata: any record or selection description, title, display name, note or label;
- `registry_detail_text` and any message text in failure evidence (diagnostic only; no decision is derived from it);
- statement bookkeeping that is not a binding member: `issued_at`, `valid_from`, `valid_until`, `selector_ref`, `revocation_ref`, `decision_ref`, `supersedes`, `participation`, signatures;
- the values inside an effective policy payload (for example an allow-list entry), which are governed by their own family rules (contract 2: 1 to 128 characters, no control, separator or `*` characters) and are escaped, never rejected, by the serializer (vector `EP-006`);
- strings inside the trust snapshot (`issuer_id`, `authority` entries and so on), which are governed by the snapshot rules and escaped by the serializer (vector `TF-003`).

The scope tokens of a signed statement are **not** exempt: the three scope members are binding identifiers, and a statement scope token that the statement validator accepts but the grammar rejects is handled by the projection rule of section 5.6.

If a component wants to record a hash of human-readable metadata as evidence, that hash is a separate evidence value outside this binding; it must never be added as a member or mixed into `binding_digest`. This document defines no such hash.

## 4. Two forms of the object and when an identity is complete

Contract 4.2: the registry produces the object with `activation_ref = null`; the enforcing gate fills `activation_ref` from its own log; the claim-carried form requires a non-null `activation_ref`; **the digest is always computed over the completed form.** Contract 4.1 says no `null` appears in a completed binding. These are consistent only if there are two forms, which this document makes explicit:

| Form | `activation_ref` | Produced by | `binding_digest` |
|---|---|---|---|
| Registry form (incomplete) | `null` | the Registry projection from verified inputs | **never computed** |
| Completed form | `sha256:` + 64 lowercase hex | the enforcing gate | computed over the completed form only |

A null `activation_ref` in a form presented for digesting, carriage or guard comparison is refused (`BI-NULL-ACTIVATION-REF`, `field_type_invalid`). Completion changes exactly one member; the vectors `BV-001` (completed) and the member-mutation control prove that completion changes the digest.

## 5. Serialization rules (exact)

These restate contract 4.1 (pinned) and fix what it leaves implicit. Where this section says more than 4.1, the extra is a ruling (sections 5.2, 5.6 and 5.7) or is listed in section 11.

### 5.1 Identifier grammar (Architect ACCEPTED)

The eight identifier members (`record_id`, `selection_id`, `family`, `consumer_id`, `operation`, `scope.tenant`, `scope.institution`, `scope.authority_domain`) must match, on the raw string:

```
\A[A-Za-z0-9][A-Za-z0-9._:-]{0,127}\Z
```

- The classes are explicit ASCII ranges: no `\w`, `\d`, locale, `re.IGNORECASE` or any Unicode-aware class. Use end-of-string (`\Z`, or full-match), never `$`: Python `$` accepts a trailing newline (`BI-TRAILING-NEWLINE`; the test asserts the hazard).
- 1 to 128 characters; the first is `A-Z`, `a-z` or `0-9`; the rest are those plus `.`, `_`, `:`, `-`.
- ASCII only. **Unsupported Unicode is rejected, never normalized**: no NFC or NFKC, no case folding, no trimming, no transcoding, no silent rewriting, no replacement. Fullwidth forms, homoglyphs (Cyrillic, Greek, U+2010), case-fold hazards (Kelvin sign U+212A, dotless i U+0131, long s U+017F), NFC and NFD forms of the same text, zero-width and byte-order-mark characters, NBSP, NUL, newline, DEL, `*`, `/` and `%` are all refused `field_invalid`. Both NFC and NFD of the same text are refused, so no component can conflate them.
- Case is significant and preserved: `tenant-a` and `Tenant-a` are two valid, distinct identifiers with different digests (`BV-005`).
- Comparison anywhere (guard, evidence, tests) is exact byte equality of the strings.
- This is a V1 interoperability restriction on canonical identity members only (section 3.2 lists what it does not cover). The Registry statement validators still accept Unicode scope tokens; where that statement is projected into a binding, section 5.6 applies.

### 5.2 Value rules and per-field integer ranges (ruling OQ-1)

Integers are **strict bounded integers with an explicit range per field**. No field borrows another field's domain: the freshness-age ceiling is a separate technical limit, epochs and counters have their own monotone domains, and the entries below are independent rows that happen to be equal today. A value must be a JSON integer in base 10, no sign (except a minus that is then out of range), leading zero, fraction or exponent, not `bool`, not a string, and inside the row's range. Refusals: outside the range, zero or negative is `field_invalid`; `bool`, string or `null` is `field_type_invalid`; a float or exponent literal is `json_number_unsupported` (parse).

### 5.3 Canonical JSON and byte encoding

`canonical_bytes` is exactly the registry profile (`registry_successor/common.py` `canonical_bytes`, quoted by contract 4.1): JSON text, object keys sorted, separators `,` and `:` with no whitespace, `ensure_ascii=True`, encoded as UTF-8.

- **Field ordering.** Keys are sorted by Unicode code point (identical to byte order for the ASCII key names) at every nesting level including `scope`. The declared order in section 3 is documentation only and never affects the bytes. The sorted top-level order is: `activation_ref`, `binding_profile`, `consumer_id`, `effective_policy_digest`, `family`, `operation`, `record_digest`, `record_id`, `record_version`, `scope`, `selection_digest`, `selection_epoch`, `selection_id`, `trust_snapshot_fingerprint`, `trust_snapshot_version`; `scope` sorts `authority_domain`, `institution`, `tenant`.
- **String escaping (needed for non-binding values such as snapshot strings).** `"` is `\"`, `\` is `\\`, U+0008, U+000C, U+000A, U+000D, U+0009 are `\b`, `\f`, `\n`, `\r`, `\t`; every other code point outside U+0020 to U+007E, **including U+007F (DEL)**, is written `\uXXXX` with **lowercase** hex; code points above U+FFFF are written as a UTF-16 surrogate pair of two `\uXXXX` escapes. `/` is not escaped. A serializer that emits raw UTF-8 (the Control Plane commitment profile uses `ensure_ascii=False`) differs for any non-ASCII value (`TF-003` carries the divergent bytes as a negative control). Binding identifiers and digests are pure ASCII from the permitted set, so they never need an escape and the two profiles coincide for a valid binding (contract 4.1).
- Integers are written as base-10 digits only. No trailing newline, no byte-order mark. The output is 7-bit ASCII (the largest valid vector, `BV-003`, is 1747 bytes).
- **Ordering by code point, and which orderings are refused (reviews S-3, SF-3).** Every ordering this profile defines is by **Unicode code point, which equals UTF-8 byte order**, never by UTF-16 code unit: the object keys of every canonical serialization (including an F5 class-name map, whose keys are open tokens, vectors `EP-016`), the sorted lists of section 6 and 7, and the "sorted path order" of the validation phases of section 5.5. The two orders differ for a code point above U+FFFF against one in U+E000 to U+FFFF (`U+FF21` sorts before `U+10400` by code point; JavaScript's default `Array.prototype.sort` and `Object.keys(...).sort()` and Java `String.compareTo` order them the other way and produce different canonical bytes and a different digest). What happens to an input that is not in order depends on the list, and is stated once here:
  - **Refused (`field_invalid`)**: the effective-payload lists of section 6 (`allowed_*`), when not in code point order or with a duplicate (vectors `EP-015`, `EI-016`). The input order is a claim about the data, so it is checked, never repaired.
  - **Normalized, never refused for order**: object keys at every level (key order in the input JSON is irrelevant, section 5.3 "Recompute, never relay") and every array of the **trust snapshot** of section 7 (`issuers`, `authority`, `revoked_key_ids`, `revoked_selections`, `min_epochs`): the fingerprint is computed over the sorted form (`TF-002`, and `TF-005` for the non-BMP / BMP-high pair in `revoked_key_ids` and `authority`). A **duplicate** in a snapshot array is refused `trust_snapshot_invalid`, not `field_invalid`.
  The Node check uses a code point comparator for object keys and for the effective-payload lists (`Object.keys(v).sort()` fails `EP-016`; a UTF-16 key sort fails `EP-016` and `TF-005` in the Python reference too). The trust snapshot arrays are sorted by the Python reference only: Node serializes the already sorted `expected_normalized`, so the `TF-005` order is checked by Python alone (review N-3).
- **Recompute, never relay.** A component recomputes `canonical_bytes` from the decoded members and never hashes received text. Input JSON may use any standard escapes or key order and any insignificant whitespace; the decoded members determine the bytes (`BV-011`, `BV-012`, `BV-013` all equal `BV-001`). A duplicate key at any depth is refused even with an equal value (`BI-DUP-TOPLEVEL-SAME`), including a duplicate revealed only after unescaping the key name (`BI-DUP-ESCAPED-KEY`), a duplicate inside an object nested in an array (`BI-DUP-NESTED-IN-ARRAY`) and a key written once as an escaped surrogate pair and once raw (`BI-DUP-ESCAPED-PAIR-VS-RAW`: the pair is one code point). Keys that are **lone** surrogate escapes (`\ud800`, `\ud801`) are distinct code units, never a duplicate (`BI-LONE-SURROGATE-KEYS-DISTINCT`); a parser that replaces an invalid surrogate with U+FFFD would see a duplicate and is not conforming.

### 5.4 Digest construction and domain separation

```
binding_digest = "sha256:" + lowercase_hex( SHA-256( P || canonical_bytes(binding) ) )
P = the 30 bytes of the ASCII text  ocs-reg/r08-v2/policy-binding  followed by one LF (0x0a)
P (hex) = 6f63732d7265672f7230382d76322f706f6c6963792d62696e64696e670a
```

The prefix is not JSON, not part of `canonical_bytes`, and is never emitted. Hashing without the prefix, with the prefix minus its newline, or with a changed prefix yields a different digest (each is asserted by the mutation control). Each digest has its own prefix: `effective_policy_digest` uses `E` (section 6) and `scope_key_digest` uses `S` (section 5.7); the three prefixes are distinct. Only `trust_snapshot_fingerprint` stays a plain registry `digest` value (`"sha256:" + hex(SHA-256(canonical_bytes(x)))`, no prefix) per contract 5 (pinned).

### 5.5 Validation order and refusal codes

A multi-defect input is refused with the first failing check in this fixed order, so every implementation reports the same code and member for the same input (`BI-MULTI-*` vectors fix the order):

1. parse; 2. document is an object; `scope` is an object; 3. member set, missing first, in sorted path order (code point order, section 5.3) (`scope.*` paths are `scope.authority_domain`, `scope.institution`, `scope.tenant`); 4. member set, unknown; 5. member types, in sorted path order; 6. member values, in sorted path order (code point order, section 5.3).

**The top-level member set and the `scope` member set are two separate sets, checked before anything is combined (review of PR #30, Copilot).** The `scope.<name>` paths are a reporting convention for members of the nested object, not member names. A top-level member whose name is literally `scope.tenant` (or any other name that is not one of the 15 listed top-level names) is an **unlisted top-level member** (`field_unknown`, member reported as that literal name), whatever its value and wherever it appears in the key order; it never stands in for, fills, overrides or is validated as the nested member. When the nested `tenant` is also absent the nested member is **missing** (checked first, `field_missing`, member `scope.tenant`); when `scope` itself is absent the single missing member is `scope` (a literal top-level `scope.*` member does not make `scope` present). An implementation that flattens before checking the member sets would let the literal key impersonate the nested one and make the verdict depend on key order; the vectors `BI-SCOPE-LITERAL-DOT-KEY-*`, `BI-SCOPE-LITERAL-DOT-KEYS-WITHOUT-SCOPE` and `BI-SCOPE-NESTED-UNKNOWN-DOTTED` pin every case, and the reference rejects the flat-merge mutant.

No new codes are introduced: every refusal below is an entry of the 48-code `CODES_V2` map (section 8). Contract 4.1 names `field_invalid` explicitly only for identifiers; the other assignments were proposed from existing codes and are now ruled (OQ-2) into the typed mapping of section 8.1, which also assigns the verdict class and the structured evidence fields. Guard-state causes that have no existing code are listed once, in section 8.2, never by repurposing an existing code.

### 5.5.1 Parse-layer precedence (normative, review S-1)

The parse codes are ordered by one rule that does not depend on how a parser streams, so every implementation reports the same code for the same input, including an input with several parse defects:

1. **Input boundary** (section 5.5.2): `input_not_text`, then `input_too_large`.
2. **Syntax of the whole text**: `json_malformed`. The grammar is RFC 8259 extended only by the tokens `NaN`, `Infinity` and `-Infinity`, which are recognized so they can be refused as number literals (step 3). A leading zero, a trailing comma, trailing text after the document, a truncated document and a byte order mark are syntax errors. An integer literal of **any length** is valid syntax.
3. **Number literals**: any float, exponent, `NaN` or `Infinity` token anywhere in the document: `json_number_unsupported`.
4. **Duplicate object keys** at any depth, compared after unescaping: `json_duplicate_key`.
5. **Nesting**: a value at depth greater than 8: `json_nesting_too_deep`.

**The scans of steps 2 to 4 are not bounded by recursion depth or by a digit limit (review SF-1, SF-2).** Steps 2, 3 and 4 run over the **whole** text, whatever its depth up to the 65536-byte limit (a 32768-level array is within the limit): an implementation must scan syntax, number literals and duplicate keys without being stopped by its own recursion cap, and may report `json_nesting_too_deep` only after steps 2 to 4 have found nothing. A parser with a recursion cap (a recursive-descent parser, `serde_json` at its default 128) therefore needs an explicit-stack scan, or a separate non-recursive pre-pass for steps 2 to 4, before it applies the depth rule; mapping its stack-overflow error to `json_nesting_too_deep` directly is not conforming. Vectors `IBND-DEEP-UNCLOSED-9000` (`json_malformed`), `IBND-DEEP-10000` (`json_nesting_too_deep`), `IBND-DEEP-10000-FLOAT` (`json_number_unsupported`), `IBND-DEEP-10000-TRAILING` (`json_malformed`), `IBND-DEEP-10000-DUP` (`json_duplicate_key`), `IBND-DEEP-AT-SIZE-LIMIT` (32768 levels, `json_nesting_too_deep`) and `IBND-DEEP-OVER-SIZE-LIMIT` (`input_too_large`) pin this; the reference scanner is iterative (an explicit stack) and the test repeats the deep vectors with a lowered Python recursion limit.

Consequences, each pinned by a `parse order` vector: `{"a":1,"a":2} x` is `json_malformed` (a parser whose object hook fires when the object closes reports the duplicate first); `{"a":1,"a":2.5}` is `json_number_unsupported`; a duplicate beside deep nesting is `json_duplicate_key`. An integer literal of **any** length is not a parse defect: it is refused by the field range as `field_invalid` at its member (`BI-INT-5000-DIGITS`, and the generated `IBND-INT-20-DIGITS`, `-21-DIGITS`, `-700-DIGITS`, `-700-DIGITS-NEGATIVE`, `-3999-DIGITS`, `-4001-DIGITS`), and the outcome must not depend on a runtime digit limit: CPython raises `ValueError` from `int()` above 4300 digits by default and above a configured limit as low as 640 (`sys.set_int_max_str_digits`, `PYTHONINTMAXSTRDIGITS`), which must never become `json_malformed` or a crash. Every field range is at most 2^53 - 1 (16 digits), so the reference classifies a literal by **length**: a literal of at most 20 digits is converted exactly, a longer one is treated as out of range without conversion (its sign is irrelevant, because both signs are out of range). The test varies the interpreter digit limit over 0, 640, 700, 4000 and 4300 across every integer-literal vector. The shipped registry loader (`registry_successor/common.py` `load_document`) follows streaming order and maps the digit-limit error to `json_malformed`; it therefore differs from this rule on multi-defect parse inputs, including every document whose float, `NaN` or `Infinity` token precedes a later syntax error (`{"a":2.5} x`, `[1.5, ]` and `[1.5` give `json_number_unsupported` there and `json_malformed` here; `BI-PARSE-ORDER-FLOAT-THEN-TRAILING` is such a case), and the later source PR must not reuse it unchanged for binding documents (section 11, item 12). After the parse layer, the phases of the first paragraph of this section apply.

**RULED (Architect Batch 6 rulings, N-10): syntax-first parsing is accepted for the canonical v2 identity boundary.** `load_document` is not reused silently where its behaviour differs from this contract. The boundary requires: (1) **deterministic byte-level parsing** (strict UTF-8, no byte order mark stripping, one parse order for every implementation); (2) **duplicate-member rejection** at any depth after unescaping; (3) **rejection of ambiguous keys** (a key that is the same member under another escape form, or a literal dotted key that could impersonate a nested member, is a duplicate or an unknown member, never a second spelling of a known one); (4) **strict numeric parsing** (no float, exponent, `NaN`, `Infinity`, leading zero or negative zero, no conversion of an over-long literal); (5) **bounded input size** (65536 UTF-8 bytes, checked first); (6) **canonical serialization** (section 5.3, exact bytes); and (7) **no coercion of malformed values** (a string, boolean or `null` is never read as an integer, and a refused value is never repaired). `trust_snapshot_version` is a strict integer in [1, 2^53 - 1] (section 5.2) written as exact canonical JSON integer text; this is an interoperable V1 representation bound and **not an operational freshness bound** (no age or recency is implied by it). The vector set `n10_input_contract` maps each of the seven requirements to existing vectors and adds the `TSV-*` refusals for `trust_snapshot_version` (0, -1, 2^53, `7.0`, `7e0`, `07`, `"7"`, `true`, `null`), and the test requires every listed vector to exist and to refuse with its recorded code and member. **RULED (Architect final ruling, N-10(c)): the snapshot-version representation bound is applied BEFORE the snapshot is used in authorization or projection.** A delivered `TrustSnapshot.version` that is not a strict integer in [1, 2^53 - 1] (exact canonical JSON integer text, no coercion) is refused at ingest, before the floor comparison, the fingerprint, any authority evaluation and any projection: an unsupported representation never reaches authority evaluation. The refusal reuses the existing `trust_snapshot_invalid` (the N-11 invalid-input refusal), stage `ingest`, member `trust_snapshot_version`; the ruling fixes the ordering, so this code and stage name is a **PROPOSED** mapping for the Architect to confirm (row `MP-trust_snapshot_invalid`). The vector set `snapshot_version_ingest_bound` (`SIB-001` to `SIB-013`) pins it, and the test requires that an authority probe is not called for any refused representation. The projection-stage refusal of section 5.6 stays as a second line of defence only. The shipped verifier (`registry_successor/verify.py`, `version >= 1` only) does not yet apply the bound; that is a source gap for the source PRs, not a contract position.

### 5.5.2 Input boundary (normative, review S-2)

The document is **text**. The byte vectors (`input_hex`, and the generated `input_boundary` vectors) express the text as UTF-8 bytes.

- Bytes must be strict UTF-8 (no overlong form, no encoded surrogate, no truncated sequence), otherwise `input_not_text`. A text-typed (`str`) entry point cannot receive invalid bytes, so it applies the same strict decode before its API and refuses with the same code; a `str` that cannot be encoded as UTF-8 (a lone surrogate) is `input_not_text`; any other type is `input_not_text`. `bytes` is accepted **only as the harness expression of text** (the vectors give UTF-8 bytes); a `bytes` object passed to a `str`-only API is `input_not_text`, as `load_document` returns for `b"{}"`, so an API that is `str`-only agrees with `load_document` after the same strict decode. A well formed JSON escape such as `\ud800` is **text**, not an encoding error: it decodes to a value that fails its member rule (`IBND-ESCAPED-LONE-SURROGATE`).
- The size limit is **65536 UTF-8 bytes of the whole text**, counted before parsing (`IBND-LIMIT-OK` at 65536, `IBND-OVER-LIMIT` at 65537, and `IBND-MULTIBYTE-OVER-LIMIT`, which is under 65536 characters but over 65536 bytes). The size limit is checked **before** any scan, so a 32769-level document is `input_too_large`, never a depth or syntax result (`IBND-DEEP-OVER-SIZE-LIMIT`).
- A byte order mark is **not** stripped: `EF BB BF` before the document is `json_malformed` (`BI-BOM-PREFIX`, `IBND-BOM-NOT-STRIPPED`).
- Depth: the deepest value, scalar leaves included, may be at depth 8 and no deeper (`IBND-DEPTH-8-*` pass, `IBND-DEPTH-9-*` refuse; this equals the shipped `_depth_ok`).

### 5.6 Projection-stage refusals: scope tokens and every other verified-input projection (ruling OQ-4, generalized after review S-5)

**Scope of this section.** A *projection-stage refusal* is any refusal that arises when a verified input (a signed statement, a record pin, a trust snapshot) is projected into a binding member. Five kinds exist today, in three groups: a statement scope token the grammar cannot carry (below); a verified trust snapshot version above 2^53 - 1 (**defence in depth only**: under the N-10(c) ruling the representation bound is applied at ingest, section 5.5.2, so a conforming verifier refuses it first; a component that reaches projection with such a version still refuses `field_invalid`, stage `projection`, member `trust_snapshot_version`; vectors `PSR-001` to `PSR-003`); and the three refusals of the derivation of `effective_policy_digest` (section 6), which is also a binding member projected from verified input (the authenticated record's family plus the caller policy): `family_unsupported`, `contract_unsupported` and an effective-payload `field_invalid`. The kinds are listed in `profile.projection_stage.kinds` of the vector file (`scope_token`, `trust_snapshot_version`, `effective_family_unsupported`, `effective_contract_unsupported`, `effective_payload_field_invalid`). **Every** projection-stage refusal, present or added later, has exactly the participation effects below: no binding for any participation, required refuses, optional never falls back by the refusal itself, never `overlay_not_applied` or `ACCEPT` by itself. The effect takes no refusal kind and no member: it is a function of participation, activation state and optional-state determination only (an implementer cannot treat a non-scope member, or a refused effective derivation, as an ordinary refusal and fall back to the caller policy). **The effect is joined to each refusal kind in data** (vectors `projection_effect_matrix`, 30 rows = 5 kinds x the six participation, state and determination cases of `scope_projection_effects`, each row carrying the kind, its registry code and the expected outcome; the test requires one outcome per case whatever the kind, so the prose claim is a data claim). **An unknown participation is refused**, never read as optional: only the exact, case-sensitive values `required` and `optional` are valid; anything else, a missing value or an empty value is `field_invalid` at member `participation` (`projection_effect_invalid`, `PEI-001` to `PEI-003`; the reference raises, so the rule survives `python -O`). The ingest bound at 2^53 - 1 is **required**, not an alternative (N-10(c), section 5.5.2): a conforming verifier refuses the version before authority evaluation and projection, and projection-only enforcement is not conforming; the projection rule above is defence in depth only.

The Registry statement validators accept scope tokens (`tenant`, `institution`, `authority_domain`) that are 1 to 128 characters with no control, separator or `*` characters; that admits Unicode and ASCII characters such as `/`, `%`, `@` and a leading `_`. The binding identifiers are ASCII-grammar only (section 5.1). The projection from a verified statement scope into the binding `scope` is therefore **identity or refusal, nothing else**:

1. If a scope token is a string, is ASCII and matches the identifier grammar, it is copied into the binding **byte for byte**.
2. Otherwise binding creation is **refused**: `field_invalid`, layer `shape`, stage `projection`, member `scope.<name>` (the first failing member in sorted path order: `scope.authority_domain`, `scope.institution`, `scope.tenant`).
3. A refused projection produces **no binding for any participation**: `field_invalid`, stage `projection`, member `scope.<name>`, and the failure evidence carries `registry_code = field_invalid`, `stage = projection`, the member, the `original_selection_digest` and the `projection_refusal_code`. A scope that cannot be represented unambiguously under the binding contract is **never silently narrowed, normalized or suppressed**. What the consumer does with the refusal depends on participation and is **not** a shortcut:
   - **REQUIRED** participation: the effect is refused (decision kind `required_policy_unavailable`). The verdict is `REFUSE` because invalidity is established; it is `UNKNOWN_FAIL_CLOSED` (HOLD) only when the consumer's authoritative state is itself indeterminate. It never falls back to the caller policy.
   - **OPTIONAL** participation: the projection refusal is **never itself an authorization or a fallback**. The consumer may fall back only through its authenticated, explicitly authorized optional-state determination (contract 4.3 and 7.4, `EXT-S4-2`), and only when the durable activation state permits optional absence (not when an activation or a required ratchet exists, and not when the state is indeterminate). The fallback must be recorded as an explicit absence or withdrawal evidence record that carries the original projection refusal code (`field_invalid`), so the absence is represented truthfully. The decision kind the determination yields is decided there, not here; contract 7.4 line 8(c) names a withdrawn row and a row past its verified `valid_until` as fallback reasons; the expiry half is superseded (Amendment 1, S-15). Under the N-12 ruling below, only a **withdrawn** row maps onto a permitted basis (a valid explicit withdrawal or fallback transition that does not revive previous authority), so `selection_withdrawn` is the only registry code that keeps an optional branch in section 8.1. **RULED (Architect final ruling, N-12(c)): `selection_expired` does not establish eligibility for optional fallback.** A definitive expiry refuses the expired selection (`REFUSE`) for every participation. If the applicable authoritative state is unavailable the verdict is `UNKNOWN_FAIL_CLOSED`. Only an independently authenticated never-required state or a valid withdrawn state is a fallback basis, and that state is decided on its own code (`selection_withdrawn`), never on the expiry: no determination basis converts a `selection_expired` outcome into a fallback. `OFD-015` to `OFD-021` pin it. `never_activated` has no registry code.
   - **RULED (Architect Batch 6 rulings, N-12): a refused projection does not establish `never_activated`.** A refused projection creates no state row, but the absence of a row that results from the refusal is not evidence that the scope never required governed policy, and it never justifies a legacy or caller-policy fallback; an unprojectable binding must not be used to reach the optional fallback. **Optional fallback is allowed only** on authenticated, consumer-owned state that **independently** establishes that the scope never required governed policy (basis `never_required_governed_policy`), or on a **valid explicit withdrawal or fallback transition that does not revive previous authority** (basis `valid_withdrawal_fallback_transition`). **REQUIRED participation fails closed.** **An unknown store is `UNKNOWN_FAIL_CLOSED` (HOLD) for every participation and is never `overlay_not_applied`**, because unknown durable state establishes nothing about the scope. **`family_unsupported` and `contract_unsupported` are `REFUSE`** (a definitive unsupported policy request) and are not optional fallback conditions unless a **separate authenticated non-participation determination** (basis `authenticated_non_participation`) exists. The vector set `optional_fallback_determination` (21 vectors, `OFD-001` to `OFD-021`) and the reference function `optional_fallback` encode these rules and the test exhausts every combination of kind, participation, store, basis, authentication, independence and revival. In the six-case matrix of `projection_effect_matrix`, the determination `authenticated` means a determination with one of these independent bases (for the two derivation kinds above, the non-participation basis).
   - **RULED (Architect final ruling, N-10(c)).** A verified snapshot version above 2^53 - 1 is not a property of one statement: it affects every scope at once. The ruling applies the representation bound at ingest (section 5.5.2), so it is refused before any authority evaluation or projection and never becomes a per-scope projection refusal in a conforming implementation.
   - **An unprojectable scope never yields `overlay_not_applied` or any `ACCEPT` by itself.** This document does not use `overlay_not_applied` as a verdict for a projection refusal anywhere (spec, vectors, mapping, tests); it remains the contract's decision kind for the authenticated optional-state determination only.
4. **Never** transliterate, normalize (NFC, NFKC, NFD, NFKD), case-fold, trim, truncate to 128 characters, percent-encode or hash a scope token into something that looks like an equivalent identifier. Any such mapping needs a separately defined collision-resistant encoding contract, and this document defines none. NFC and NFD forms of the same text are two different refused values.
5. **Provenance.** The original statement scope bytes stay in the signed statement, which the binding commits through the `selection_digest` member (the digest of the canonical statement, scope included). Nothing re-encodes them and no separate provenance digest is defined (none could be defined without a second preimage for the same bytes). A verified statement with an unprojectable scope remains a verified statement.

Vectors `scope_projection_effects` (6, ruling N-5; the six cases are also the columns of `projection_effect_matrix`): required with known state (`REFUSE`), required with indeterminate state (`UNKNOWN_FAIL_CLOSED`), optional with an authenticated determination and a permitting activation state (the only fallback, with the absence or withdrawal evidence carrying `field_invalid`), optional where the activation state forbids absence (`REFUSE`), optional without an authenticated determination (`REFUSE`) and optional with indeterminate state (`UNKNOWN_FAIL_CLOSED`); the test also exhausts every participation, state and determination combination and requires that no combination yields `overlay_not_applied` or an `ACCEPT` and that exactly one combination permits a fallback. Vectors `scope_projection` (17): ASCII scopes projected unchanged (`PJ-001` to `PJ-003`); Unicode tokens in a statement the existing validator accepts, refused (NFC, NFD, Cyrillic, fullwidth, Kelvin sign, CJK, non-BMP, ligature, 100-character CJK, two bad members); ASCII tokens outside the grammar (`/`, `%`, leading `_`, `@`). Every refused vector records `projection_refusal_code`, `yields_overlay_not_applied: false` and `yields_accept: false` (it no longer carries a per-participation decision). Every vector records `statement_validator_accepts: true` (the test re-implements the statement token rule and asserts it) and the original scope bytes as hex. The test asserts that several refused tokens would become valid identifiers under NFKC, case folding or truncation, which is exactly what the rule forbids.

### 5.7 Scope key (ruling OQ-5)

Contract 7.4 line 2 and the consumer state (contract 7, "one row per scope key") need one key that Runtime, Control Plane and evidence all derive identically. The identifier grammar allows `:`, `.`, `_` and `-`, so any delimiter-joined or concatenated key is ambiguous (a consumer `a:b` with tenant `c` against a consumer `a` with tenant `b:c`). The scope key is therefore a **versioned canonical JSON object**, with the same canonical rules as section 5.3:

```
scope_key        = canonical_bytes({
                     "scope_key_profile": "ocs-scope-key/1",
                     "tenant": ..., "institution": ..., "authority_domain": ...,
                     "consumer_id": ..., "operation": ..., "family": ..., "selection_id": ... })
scope_key_digest = "sha256:" + lowercase_hex( SHA-256( S || scope_key ) )
S = the ASCII text  ocs-reg/r08-v2/scope-key  followed by one LF (0x0a)
S (hex) = 6f63732d7265672f7230382d76322f73636f70652d6b65790a
```

Every member is a quoted JSON string under its own key, so tuple boundaries are preserved structurally and no concatenation or delimiter collision is possible; keys sort by code point, so the member order never matters. The members are **exactly the seven the contract's own scope-key definition lists** (contract 7.4 line 2; the conformance kit uses the same seven), each an identifier of section 5.1:

| Member | Why it is in the key |
|---|---|
| `tenant`, `institution`, `authority_domain` | the enforcing scope of the verified statement (binding `scope`); prevents cross-tenant, cross-institution and cross-authority-domain substitution |
| `consumer_id` | the enforcing component; the same policy at another component is another scope |
| `operation` | the governed operation; a selection for one operation never governs another |
| `family` | the policy family; prevents substituting one family's selection for another's |
| `selection_id` | the selection identity whose epochs succeed one another; epochs of one `selection_id` keep continuity on one row, a different `selection_id` is a different row |

**Deliberately not members** (each would orphan the row at every transition and make the high-water and continuity impossible, or is not scope identity): `selection_epoch` (the high-water compares it **against** the row), `selection_digest`, `record_id`, `record_version`, `record_digest` (a new record version under the same selection is a transition of the same row), `participation` (the ratchet is per scope key), `activation_ref` (produced by activation of the row), `effective_policy_digest` (caller-dependent evidence), `trust_snapshot_version` and `trust_snapshot_fingerprint`, `valid_from`, `valid_until`, the issuer key id, and `binding_profile`. No identifier is added that a legitimate successor statement would change, and none of the seven is dropped, because contract 7.4 names all seven.

`scope_key` (the canonical bytes) is the identity; `scope_key_digest` is a compact index and evidence value (`ocs-registry-failure-detail/1` carries it as `scope_key_digest`). A store must not derive the key from a delimiter-joined string. The conformance kit's local `scope_key` string (a JSON array of the same seven in a fixed order, unversioned and not domain-separated) is a model-only rendering; `EXT-S4-0` adopts this profile and the mapping between the two is one-to-one. A key derived from a completed binding (`scope.*`, `consumer_id`, `operation`, `family`, `selection_id`) validates for every valid binding vector.

Vectors (`scope_keys`, 12; `scope_key_collision_pairs`, 6; `scope_keys_invalid`, 13): `SK-001` derived from `BV-001` (digest `sha256:0060b8e5d3b226b2bd206606bd32ccb8b2356da3ce7317ef63a0d755fbb9ea31`); a reversed-order twin equal to it; one single-member mutation per member (`SK-MUT-*`, 7), each with a different key; a case variant; minimum and maximum lengths. The six collision pairs (`SKC-001` to `SKC-006`) cover the colon, dot, hyphen, underscore and no-delimiter cases at several member boundaries; for each pair the naive join of the same members is **equal**, while the canonical keys and digests **differ** (the test asserts both, plus an exhaustive 125-tuple check). Invalid keys refuse a missing or extra member (an added `selection_epoch` or `record_version` is `field_unknown`), a wrong profile, non-ASCII or empty members, wrong types, a duplicate key and a non-object.

## 6. `effective_policy_digest` (ruling OQ-3)

The effective policy digest is **claim-carried evidence about the policy the enforcing gate actually applied**. It is derived from the canonical serialized **effective** policy data, which already includes every applicable narrowed value (the composed `min(caller, registry)` values and per-dimension intersections), plus the versioned policy context. It is **not** derived from mutable display metadata, selection bookkeeping or anything a caller can relabel. The preimage is deterministic, domain-separated and reproducible by every consumer from the vectors.

```
effective_policy_digest = "sha256:" + lowercase_hex( SHA-256( E || canonical_bytes(preimage) ) )
E = the ASCII text  ocs-reg/r08-v2/effective-policy  followed by one LF (0x0a)
E (hex) = 6f63732d7265672f7230382d76322f6566666563746976652d706f6c6963790a
preimage = {
  "effective_policy_profile": "ocs-effective-policy/1",
  "contract_version":         "r08-v2",
  "family":                   <the binding `family` identifier>,
  "family_version":           <DERIVED, never an input: the registered family table tag (contract section 2) looked up by the authenticated record's family id and contract_version: "v1" for f1a, "v2" for f1ab, f4, f5>,
  "deny_all":                 <sorted list of the payload dimensions whose effective set is the explicit empty set; [] for F4 and F5>,
  "effective":                <the composed effective payload: exactly the family payload keys of contract section 2, nothing else>
}
```

- `effective` holds, for `f1ab-local-execution-allowlist`, the five sorted, duplicate-free lists `allowed_actions`, `allowed_adapters`, `allowed_units`, `allowed_institutions`, `allowed_authority_domains` (an explicit deny-all dimension is `[]`; an **omitted** dimension refuses `field_missing`, so omitted never means empty); for `f1a-local-execution-allowlist` the first three; for `f4-control-plane-age-bounds` the two composed integers; for `f5-action-class-age-bounds` the composed per-class map. List entries follow contract 2 (1 to 128 code points, no `*`, no code point excluded by the frozen token table of section 6.1, exact strings, at most 256 entries) and are escaped, never rejected, by the serializer. Integers follow the age rows of section 5.2 (effective range 0 to 2147483647).
- **F5 class vocabulary (review N-i).** The `f5-action-class-age-bounds` map keys are validated only as contract section 2 tokens by this reference; this document defines no class vocabulary, so the effective digest accepts any token class name. The contract's "unknown action class refuses" rule needs the Control Plane's class table and is exercised by the consumer, not by these vectors.
- `deny_all` is redundant by construction (derived from `effective`) and is checked: a presented preimage whose flags do not equal the empty dimensions is refused `field_invalid` (`EPI-004`, `EPI-006`). It makes the explicit empty-set decision visible in the preimage and keeps "explicit deny-all" distinct from any absent value.
- **`contract_version` keying (review S-4).** An effective-policy identity exists for `contract_version` `r08-v2` **only**, for every family. The shipped `FAMILIES` table (`registry_successor/families.py`) also registers `f1a` for `r08-v1`, but the preimage hardwires `"r08-v2"`, so an `f1a` identity under `r08-v1` can never exist: a consumer must not reuse `lookup_for("r08-v1")` for this lookup and must key it on `r08-v2` only, through the identity module's own dispatch table (below). An unregistered `contract_version` string is `contract_unsupported` at member `contract_version` on **both** paths, the input (`EFV-016`, `EFV-020`) and a presented preimage (`EPI-002`, `EPI-008`); a non-string is `field_type_invalid` (`EPI-009`).
- **Source of `family_version` (review S-4).** No `family_version` attribute exists in `registry_successor/families.py` at `f618cdf`. The tag (`v1` for `f1a`, `v2` for `f1ab`, `f4`, `f5`) is the version column of the contract section 2 family table; the later `EXT-S1-5` source PR adds it as a constant registered next to each table entry, and these vectors (`EFV-*`) are the oracle until it exists. **Dispatch table owner (review SF-8).** The shipped `FAMILIES` (`f1a`) and `FAMILIES_V2` (`f1a`, `f1ab`) tables contain no `f4` or `f5` entry (those families appear only in the contract section 2 table, owner Control Plane), so a lookup in them gives `family_unsupported` for `f4-control-plane-age-bounds` and `f5-action-class-age-bounds` although the vectors (`EFV-*`, `EP-003`, `EP-005`, `EP-009`, `EP-010`) require valid identities. The identity module therefore **owns one `r08-v2` dispatch table** with exactly four rows (`f1a` -> `v1`, `f1ab` -> `v2`, `f4` -> `v2`, `f5` -> `v2`; the payload key set and the range roles of section 5.2 come with the row), and the source PR neither requires nor performs an edit of the shipped tables for `f4` and `f5`. The effective-policy identity of an `f4` or `f5` record is available independently of whether the registry itself can store such a record.
- **`family_version` is never a free input (ruling N-3).** The identity API takes exactly `{family, contract_version, effective}`; `family` and `contract_version` come from the **registered, authenticated** record, and `family_version` is **looked up** from the registered family table (contract section 2) keyed by that family id and `contract_version`. No consumer function takes `family_version` as a parameter (the test inspects the reference signatures), and a caller-supplied or payload-supplied `family_version` is refused `field_unknown` at member `family_version` whatever its value, **even the registered tag** (vectors `EFV-011` to `EFV-014`, `EFV-017`; the member-set check precedes the registered lookup). An unregistered family refuses `family_unsupported` (`EFV-015`), and a registered family under a `contract_version` not registered for it refuses `contract_unsupported` (`EFV-016`, the closest existing typed code). A presented preimage (claim evidence) is only **compared** with the preimage rebuilt from the registered table, never trusted (`EPI-001`). Validation order for the input: member set (missing, then unknown), registered lookup (family, then contract version), effective payload. A presented preimage runs the same registered lookup before its payload (profile, `family` type, `contract_version` type, registered lookup, payload, `family_version`, `deny_all`). Valid inputs `EFV-001` to `EFV-004` derive the tag and equal `EP-001`, `EP-003`, `EP-005` and `EP-014`.
- Family id, derived `family_version`, `contract_version` and the preimage profile are the **versioned policy context**: the same effective data under another family, another family version, another contract version or another preimage profile has a different digest (controls `EC-001` to `EC-004`). A family not in the dispatch table refuses (`family_unsupported`), and a presented preimage whose `family_version` is not the registered tag refuses `field_invalid` (`EPI-001`).
- **Not in the preimage:** display names, titles, descriptions or any metadata (adding one is refused `field_unknown`, `EI-006`, `EPI-005`; control `EC-007` shows it would change the digest); selection id, epoch or digest, record triple, caller policy digest, validity window, `activation_ref`, trust snapshot identity and every other binding member (each is its own binding member or consumer state). The registry and caller inputs of a composition are not in the preimage either: only the narrowed result is, so two routes to the same effective sets give the same digest (`EP-012` equals `EP-011`).
- The preimage uses the same canonical JSON rules as section 5.3 (`ensure_ascii`, sorted keys). The prefix `E` is distinct from the binding prefix and the scope-key prefix, and hashing without it, with it minus its newline or with the binding prefix gives a different digest (asserted).
- **Caller-dependent evidence, never compared to durable selection state.** Because narrowing depends on the caller policy, the digest legitimately differs per caller under one unchanged selection epoch (`EP-007` and `EP-008` share a registry payload and differ in digest). It is bound by the claim commitment and checked only for self-consistency with the claim; **the guard does not compare it to durable selection state**, as if selection state alone determined its value, and it is never authority and never widens what the selection permits (contract 4 and 7.4 line 3, unchanged).

Vectors (`effective_payloads`, 16; `effective_policy_controls`, 7; `effective_payloads_invalid`, 18; `effective_preimage_invalid`, 9; `effective_inputs`, 14: 4 valid derived-tag inputs and 10 refusals, ruling N-3): `EP-001` to `EP-006` are the original five-set, F4, F5, deny-all and non-ASCII cases under the ruled preimage (key-order twin `EP-002`); `EP-007` and `EP-008` narrowed F1ab values for two callers; `EP-009` and `EP-010` narrowed F4 and F5; `EP-011` one deny-all dimension; `EP-012` an empty intersection equal to it; `EP-013` effective 0 and the ceiling; `EP-014` the f1a family; `EP-015` and `EP-016` the code point order for lists and for class-name keys (section 5.3); `EI-017` and `EI-018` an entry or class name containing U+0020 (category Z, refused). Each narrowed vector carries its registry and caller inputs, and the test recomputes the composition independently before the digest. `BV-006`, `BV-007`, `BV-014` and `BV-015` embed the digests of `EP-003`, `EP-005`, `EP-004` and `EP-001`.

**Relation to the contract text.** Contract 4 (pinned) describes the digest as the `digest` of the canonical composed effective payload, and the contract's section 9 worked example (`BV-001`) prints a digest in that earlier form. The ruling supersedes the wording for new implementations. `BV-001` still reproduces the contract's printed `binding_digest`, because the binding digest treats `effective_policy_digest` as an opaque, format-checked digest string and never recomputes it, so the printed value stays a valid member value. The contract wording and worked example need one amendment when next edited (section 11, item 10; section 12, N-2). The `legacy_payload_only_digest_control` field of each `EP-*` vector holds the earlier payload-only digest so the difference is visible.

### 6.1 Frozen token membership: Unicode version and table (RULED, Architect Batch 6 rulings, N-13)

An installed platform Unicode database never decides authorization semantics. The token rule of contract section 2 (an effective-payload list entry, an F5 class name) is therefore frozen as follows.

- **Rule.** A token is valid iff it is 1 to 128 code points, contains no `*`, and contains no code point inside an excluded range of the pinned table. **Excluded** = every code point whose Unicode **15.1.0** general category starts with `C` (`Cc`, `Cf`, `Cs`, `Co`, `Cn`: control, format, surrogate, private use and **unassigned**) or `Z` (`Zs`, `Zl`, `Zp`). Code points assigned only after 15.1.0 are `Cn` in the table and are **refused**. No normalization (NFC, NFKC, NFD, NFKD), case folding or trimming is applied: NFC and NFD forms are two different texts, each decided on its own code points.
- **Version.** Unicode **15.1.0** is **FROZEN** as the token-property version for this bounded V1 profile (Architect final ruling, N-13). A later version is a new contract version with its own table and vectors; nothing derives the version, or any token decision, from the host runtime (Python `unicodedata`, Node ICU or `Intl`).
- **Verification of the table (what was and was not checked).** The committed table was regenerated from three sources that carry Unicode 15.1.0 and compared range for range: CPython 3.13.17 `unicodedata` (15.1.0), the PyPI package `unicodedata2` 15.1.0 (an independent C build of the 15.1.0 tables, sdist sha256 `cb30f189ad66482f8529a45da71b2a8841e9bd2bb376cc2933003a4a55a07648`) and the npm package `@unicode/unicode-15.1.0` 2.1.0 (tarball sha256 `841a96a343bfee1c7a0e12d24616038f5ad5485c76273ee6cd0bfc43ea8cea37`; union of its `General_Category/Other` and `General_Category/Separator` range files, read by Node and independent of Node's own ICU). All three produce exactly the 713 ranges and 964488 excluded code points of the table. **Not verified:** the table was not compared with the UCD text files themselves (`UnicodeData.txt`, `DerivedGeneralCategory.txt` at `unicode.org/Public/15.1.0/ucd/`), because the build environment's network allowlist refused `unicode.org` (HTTP 403); the three sources above are packaged builds of those files. Anyone with access should repeat the comparison against the UCD files (the procedure below applies unchanged). The record is in `pins.token_unicode_table.independent_verification`.
- **Table.** `docs/ext-implementation/data/ext-s1-5-token-unicode-15.1-excluded-ranges.json`: 713 ascending, non-adjacent, inclusive excluded ranges over U+0000..U+10FFFF, pinned by sha256 `8c85813d25f6ed53371e27f0e4895a89de44a619824486dcff8d48ff8b607474` (literal in the test and in the vectors, `pins.token_unicode_table`). A **full-scan pin** (`pins.token_unicode_table.full_scan`) fixes the membership of all 1114112 code points (one character per code point, `1` excluded) by its sha256 and its excluded count, so two runtimes can be compared in one value.
- **Runtimes.** Every implementation consults the table (a binary search over the ranges), **not** its own database. The Python reference (`frozen_token_valid`) and the Node cross-check both do, and the test poisons `unicodedata` to show the Python decision does not depend on it. The Node check recomputes every `token_unicode` vector and the full-scan pin from the committed table.
- **Reproducible procedure.** On a runtime whose Unicode database is exactly 15.1.0 (CPython 3.12 or 3.13 at the version shown by `unicodedata.unidata_version`), walk U+0000..U+10FFFF, mark each code point whose `unicodedata.category(chr(cp))[0]` is `C` or `Z`, and merge adjacent marked code points into inclusive ranges written as upper-case hexadecimal `[start, end]` pairs; the test regenerates the ranges this way and requires equality when the runtime is 15.1.0, and skips (with the committed table authoritative) otherwise.
- **Divergent code points.** Python 3.13 (Unicode 15.1) and Node 22.23.4 (ICU, Unicode 17.0) disagree on **at least** 5690 code points: exactly 5690 in U+0080..U+2FFFF (first U+088F, last U+2CEAD) and a further 4298 in plane 3, U+30000..U+33479 (first U+323B0), **9988 over the whole code point range**; all are unassigned in 15.1 and assigned in 17.0, none in the other direction (one comparison, not a bound for other releases). The vector set `token_unicode` (`TU-001` to `TU-018` controls, including `TU-018`, a code point assigned exactly in 15.1; `TU-D01` to `TU-D20` divergent, including two in plane 3) pins them: the frozen table refuses every one, on every runtime, including inside an otherwise valid token (`TU-D18`).
- **Ambiguous or unlisted tokens refuse.** A code point the table lists as excluded, including one unassigned in the pinned version, refuses `field_invalid`; there is no "unknown class" path that accepts.
- **Not the ASCII grammar.** The binding identifier grammar of section 5.1 is **not** substituted for this rule: permitted Unicode payload entries (for example `transf\u00e9rer`, `EP-006`) stay permitted, and `a/b`, `%` and a leading `_` stay valid tokens. The shipped registry statement validator (`valid_token`) still reads the runtime database; replacing it is a source PR matter, and until then a signed statement scope token is projected by the identity-or-refusal rule of section 5.6, which already refuses every non-ASCII token.

## 7. Trust snapshot identity

`trust_snapshot_version` is the verified `TrustSnapshot.version`. `trust_snapshot_fingerprint` is contract 5, pinned: the plain registry `digest` (no prefix) of the normalized snapshot object `{snapshot_id, version, valid_until, issuers, revoked_key_ids, revoked_selections, min_epochs}` with: `issuers` sorted by `key_id`, each `{key_id, issuer_id, alg, public_key (lowercase hex), authority}`; `authority` a list of five-element arrays `[consumer_id, tenant, institution, authority_domain, operation]` sorted lexicographically by array (code-point string comparison); `revoked_key_ids` sorted; `revoked_selections` sorted as `[selection_id, epoch]` by `(selection_id, epoch)` with the epoch compared **as an integer** (`["sel-old-1", 2]` before `["sel-old-1", 10]`, asserted by the test); `min_epochs` sorted as `[selection_id, floor]` by `selection_id`. Empty collections are present as `[]`, never omitted or null (`TF-004`). Duplicates (issuer `key_id`, `revoked_selections` pair, `min_epochs` selection id, `revoked_key_ids` entry) refuse `trust_snapshot_invalid` (`TI-001` to `TI-004`; verdict class `REFUSE` **PROPOSED**, mapping row `MP-trust_snapshot_invalid`, section 8.1). `TF-001` reproduces the contract's printed fingerprint from the contract text; `TF-002` is the unsorted-input case; the test also mutates twelve snapshot fields and requires twelve distinct fingerprints.

Every array above is **normalized** (sorted by code point, section 5.3), never refused for order; a duplicate is `trust_snapshot_invalid`. The fingerprint's input strings are not ASCII-restricted. It is computed by the Registry serializer (`ensure_ascii=True`). A component that only carries the fingerprint string never recomputes it; a component that recomputes it must use this serializer (section 5.3).

### 8.1 Typed, closed reason-code mapping (ruling OQ-2)

The **decision kind** column uses only the closed contract 3.2 set (`applied`, `applied_deny_all`, `overlay_not_applied`, `required_policy_unavailable`). Every refusal row is `required_policy_unavailable` (the required-participation kind). **RULED (Architect Batch 6 rulings, N-11).** `trust_snapshot_missing` stays **one** reason code, because both situations mean that the authoritative trust state the operation requires is unavailable, and one verdict class (`UNKNOWN_FAIL_CLOSED`). The two situations are **not** presented as equivalent underlying causes: the evidence carries a **typed, versioned stage**, `stage` = `INPUT` (no required snapshot supplied) or `DURABLE_STATE` (no applicable trusted snapshot accepted in authoritative durable state), with `stage_version` = `ocs-evidence-stage/1` (field added to the closed evidence vocabulary and to both mapping rows). A consumer must not merge the two stage values, and a new stage value or a changed meaning is a new `stage_version`. A snapshot that was supplied but is definitively malformed is the applicable invalid-input refusal (`trust_snapshot_invalid`), never `trust_snapshot_missing`. Neither stage is ever an optional fallback by itself, and neither reuses another code, so section 8.2's no-reuse rule is not weakened. The earlier stage values `verify` and `guard` are retired.

**Optional rules carried by the rows (RULED, N-12).** `MP-store_unknown` is `UNKNOWN_FAIL_CLOSED` for every participation and never `overlay_not_applied`; `MP-family_unsupported` and `MP-contract_unsupported` are `REFUSE` and no optional fallback without a separate authenticated non-participation determination (section 5.6).

The mapping is the vector set `defect_mapping` (35 rows; static table assertions checked for internal consistency, contract 3.2 vocabulary and verdict-class rules; the `trust_snapshot_missing` rows are told apart by the typed, versioned evidence stage (`INPUT`, `DURABLE_STATE`, `stage_version`) and the two optional branches are asserted; no mapping row drives a decision (the projection effect is driven by `projection_effect_matrix`); `projection_refusal_code` joins the closed evidence vocabulary, ruling N-5). ### 8.3 Policy identity members against the conformance kit (RULED, Architect Batch 6 rulings, ruling 4.3)

A policy-identity mismatch accounts for **all applicable authoritative identity members** of the **complete declared canonical binding version** (`ocs-policy-binding/1`): `scope_key`, `selection_id`, `selection_epoch`, `selection_digest`, the selected record identity (`record_id`, `record_version`, `record_digest`) and `activation_ref`. A member that a historical binding or contract version does not declare is **never manufactured and never checked** for that version (section 5.1 and the version column of the member table are the declaration). A narrower conformance test that compares fewer members does not remove a member from the canonical row. Selection epoch has its own stale or rollback interpretation: a **definitively stale** epoch (claim epoch behind the authoritative row) is `REFUSE_STALE` with `selection_epoch_stale`, **never** `policy_identity_mismatch`; only an epoch **ahead** of the row, which no authenticated state yet supports, is an identity mismatch (member `selection_epoch`).

The kit (`registry_conformance/highwater.py` `_guard`, about lines 996 to 1001, read here as source text and never imported) and this mapping agree on every overlapping case; the vector set `policy_identity_members` records the correspondence and the test ties the kit's order by source text and to kit vector `GD-02`:

| Member | Canonical row | Kit |
|---|---|---|
| `scope_key` | any difference: `policy_identity_mismatch`, member `scope_key` | the kit selects the durable row **by** the scope key (seven members, `selection_id` included): a claim whose key has no row is `not_activated` |
| `selection_id` | any difference: member `selection_id` | a member of the kit's scope key, so a miss is `not_activated`; another `selection_id` in the same chain is `selection_conflict` |
| `selection_epoch` | behind the row: `REFUSE_STALE` `selection_epoch_stale`; ahead: `policy_identity_mismatch`, member `selection_epoch` | identical (`b.selection_epoch < row.epoch` stale, then `!=` mismatch) |

The only difference is **where** the scope key and `selection_id` are detected: the kit finds them by row selection and reports the row-selection codes (`not_activated`, `selection_conflict`), because its binding carries no scope members; a consumer whose binding carries the scope separately from row selection reports `policy_identity_mismatch` with member `scope_key` or `selection_id`. The kit's own vectors still pass unchanged.

## 11. Ambiguities found in the existing contract (recorded, not resolved by fiat)

1. **Null versus complete.** Contract 4.1 forbids `null` in a completed binding; 4.2 has the Registry produce `activation_ref = null`. Reconciled here as two forms (section 4). The contract should say so in one sentence when next edited.
2. **Typed codes.** 4.1 requires "each refused with a typed code" but names only `field_invalid` (identifiers). The mapping of the other defects is ruled (OQ-2) and recorded in section 8.1.
3. **Effective payload preimage** was described in words only, with no worked example; fixed by ruling OQ-3 (section 6).
4. **Integer upper bound** is absent from 4.1 although the registry already bounds epoch and record version at 2^31 - 1; fixed per field by ruling OQ-1 (section 5.2). The snapshot version has no source ceiling at all; its bound (2^53 - 1) is component-owner-derived (ruling N-1) and is not stated in the pinned contract text.
5. **ASCII restriction versus statement validation.** The existing statement validators accept Unicode scope tokens (`valid_token`, scope fields) and lowercase 3 to 64 character identifiers for the others; 4.1 says v2 refuses non-ASCII but does not say at which layer. A signed statement with a non-ASCII tenant can verify yet cannot be projected into a binding; ruled (OQ-4) as identity-or-refusal projection (section 5.6).
6. **Card title.** The `EXT-S1-5` card heading says "46-code outcome map"; its criterion and ADR D-02 say 48 (46 plus two). This document uses 48.
7. **Stale map note.** The outcome map `meta` still says the v2 codes are `in_CODES false until EXT-S1-2 declares CODES_V2`; `CODES_V2` now exists. Historical, harmless.
8. **Control Plane serializer equivalence** is a source reading (ADR D-15), not a tested result, until `EXT-S4-4`.
9. **Age maximum still reads "pending".** Contract 2, 2.1 and ADR D-27 say `AGE_MAX` is not adopted. Ruling OQ-1 sets the technical representation ceiling 2147483647 s with the timestamp-safety qualification (section 5.2). The contract and the Control Plane table need the update when next edited; this work edits neither.
10. **Effective digest wording.** Contract 4 describes `effective_policy_digest` as the plain `digest` of the composed payload and the section 9 worked example prints a digest of that form; ruling OQ-3 requires the domain-separated preimage of section 6. `BV-001` stays valid because the binding never recomputes that member.
11. **Scope key.** Contract 7.4 line 2 lists seven members but no serialization; the conformance kit renders them as an unversioned JSON array string. Section 5.7 fixes one versioned, domain-separated form (ruling OQ-5).
12. **Parse order and the shipped loader (review S-1, S-2).** Section 5.5.1 fixes a syntax-first order. The shipped `load_document` follows streaming order (an object hook fires when the object closes, a float hook when the literal is parsed) and maps CPython's integer digit limit to `json_malformed`, so on multi-defect parse inputs (`{"a":1,"a":2} x`, a 5000-digit integer, any float token before a later syntax error such as `{"a":2.5} x`) it returns a different code. The v1 loader is frozen and its goldens are unchanged; the `EXT-S1-5` source PR must give binding documents a syntax-first pass (or its own loader) rather than reuse `load_document` unchanged. **RULED (Architect Batch 6 rulings, N-10):** syntax-first parsing is accepted (section 5.5.1).
13. **`family_version` has no source attribute (review S-4).** `registry_successor/families.py` at `f618cdf` carries no `family_version` tag, and its `FAMILIES` table registers `f1a` for `r08-v1`; an identity for `r08-v1` is refused here (section 6).
14. **Decision-kind vocabulary (review S-6).** Earlier drafts used the labels `refused` and `evaluator_denied` in the decision-kind column, which are not in the closed contract 3.2 set; section 8.1 now uses the contract's four kinds and records the evaluator denial as a non-Registry outcome.
15. **Optional scope with an unknown store (review SF-5).** Contract 8.3 item 3 (line 242) says that when the store is unknown every required scope refuses and optional scopes report `overlay_not_applied` with the cause (line 205 says the same of a gap), while the conformance README (UNKNOWN is never an optional fallback) and contract 7.4 line 8(b) (unknown store: refused) say the reverse. This document follows the README and 7.4 (optional with indeterminate state is `UNKNOWN_FAIL_CLOSED`, the fail-closed direction), so a Runtime built from the 8.3 text alone would return `overlay_not_applied` where the vectors say HOLD. **RULED (Architect Batch 6 rulings, N-12):** an unknown store is HOLD, never `overlay_not_applied` (section 5.6). The frozen contract and ADR are hash-pinned and are not edited; the conflicting sentences (contract 8.3 item 3, the contract line 205 supersession-gap sentence and the P1 plan statements that read optional as `overlay_not_applied` for every failure) are recorded as superseded in Amendment 1 (S-13, S-14) and corrected in the P1 plan documents.
17. **Optional expiry versus N-12.** Contract 7.4 line 8(c) names a row past its verified `valid_until` as an optional fallback reason. **RULED (Architect final ruling, N-12(c)):** expiry is not a fallback basis; it refuses the expired selection, and unavailable authoritative state is `UNKNOWN_FAIL_CLOSED` (section 5.6). The frozen sentence is recorded as superseded in Amendment 1 (S-15).
16. **`trust_snapshot_missing` has two meanings (review SF-4).** Contract 3.3 and `CODES` define it as "no trust snapshot was supplied" (layer `trust`); the guard uses it for "no accepted snapshot in durable state". Both map to ``UNKNOWN_FAIL_CLOSED`` in this document; the contexts are told apart by the typed, versioned evidence stage (`INPUT`, `DURABLE_STATE`). **RULED (Architect Batch 6 rulings, N-11):** one code, with the typed, versioned stage `INPUT` or `DURABLE_STATE` (section 8.1).
